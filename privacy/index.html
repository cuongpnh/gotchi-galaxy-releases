Privacy policy

Gotchi Galaxy is a pixel pet game for everyone, children included. It has no ads, no accounts and no login, and it never sells or rents anything about you. This page says exactly what the game keeps, what it sends, and how to switch things off.

Last updated: 21 October 2026. The game is made by Qifi, 234 Serangoon Ave 3, #07-74, Singapore 550234. Questions, or a request to delete something: support@gotchigalaxy.app.

What stays on your device

Your game lives on your device: your pets and their names, your player name, your rooms, your settings and a random device id. None of it is sent anywhere unless you use one of the online features below.

  • Steps ("Walk with me"), only if you turn it on: the game reads today's step count from your phone to make trips shorter. The count stays on your phone and is never sent.
  • Pictures you take with the in-game camera button only leave the device if you choose to share or save them.
  • Backups: on Android, your phone may include the game's save in its own backup to your Google account, like other apps. That is your phone's backup, which we never see.

What the game sends, and why

Everything the game sends travels encrypted (HTTPS).

Anonymous usage counts. Once a day the game sends a short summary: which parts of the game were opened and for how long, how many times things were done (for example games played or eggs hatched), how quickly our server answered, the game version and the kind of device (Android, iPhone, Mac, web). It carries a random id that changes every 30 days, and no names, no pets and no device id. We use it to see which parts of the game players enjoy. A grown-up can switch it off under Settings > Grown-ups > Share usage; nothing is counted or sent after that.

Crash reports. If the game crashes, it sends a report so we can fix the problem: what went wrong, the game version, the device model and system version, and the game's last steps before the crash. Reports do not include player or pet names. They carry your friend code (the random code Pet Plaza shows, made from the device key), so that when you write to us we can find the reports from your game. For a child (see Children below) a report carries no friend code, no device model or detail beyond the system's name, and no trail of the game's last steps: it still says what went wrong and where, and is never linked to a person. They go to Sentry, the service we use to read them.

Playing online (the town square, friends, visits, gifts, leaderboards, the community goal), only when you use it:

  • Your device has a random device key that signs what it sends, so nobody else can pretend to be you. Our server keeps it next to your leaderboard scores and your share of the community goal.
  • On the town square, other players see your pet and a small number, never your name. Messages between players are ready-made ones and stickers only: there is no chat.
  • Friends you add see your player name and the pet cards you send them. Messages to friends wait on our server for at most 7 days, and are gone as soon as they are read. A gift for you from the game's maker can wait for up to 30 days.
  • Moving your island to a new device sends it locked with a one-time code; our server cannot open it, and it is deleted after 10 minutes.
  • While the game is open and online, our server knows that your friend code is on (your friends see you as online or away), which kind of device and game version it is, and which country the connection comes from (two letters, from Cloudflare's view of the internet address). This is forgotten when the game closes.
  • Friends' messages on a closed game (phones only), only if you allow them: your phone gets a push address from Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google's service for app notifications), and our server keeps it with your friend code, together with your language, your quiet hours and how many friends may wake the phone, so a friend's message can reach you. Switching the notifications off, or removing the game, ends it. In kid mode, and for a child, this needs a grown-up's yes (Settings > Grown-ups > Child safety > Friend messages). Firebase is not started and makes no installation id or push address until this is switched on; a game that never uses friends' messages never contacts it.
  • To stop floods of fake requests, our server counts requests per internet address for a minute at a time, and for the community goal keeps a scrambled tag of the address (it cannot be turned back into the address) for at most 4 weeks.
  • Reporting a player. Every place that shows another player's name has a Report button (a friend, a friend request, a player on the Wi-Fi or the town square). It sends our server your friend code, the reported player's friend code (or, for someone met on the Wi-Fi or the square, the id they had there), the reason you picked, the name and pet names as you saw them, and the game version. Nothing is sent to the reported player. We read reports to act on them, and keep each for 1 year, then it is deleted. A child can report with no grown-up's check, and the game tells them to tell a grown-up too.
  • Settings > Friends online switches friends off.

Playing on the same Wi-Fi. Nearby players on your network can see your player name, your pet and its name. Settings > Findable hides you.

Updates and events. The game reads the list of live events (and, on Mac and the web, the list of updates) from our public pages on GitHub. Like any web page, GitHub sees the device's internet address when it is read.

Names are nicknames

The game never asks for a real name, an email, a birthday or where you live. Player and pet names are nicknames: please do not use a real full name. Names you type are only shown to friends you add and to players on your own Wi-Fi.

Who else is involved

  • Cloudflare runs our online server and stores the usage counts.
  • Sentry receives crash reports.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging delivers friends' messages to a phone whose game is closed, when that is switched on.
  • GitHub hosts the public pages the game reads.

None of them may use the data for anything but running these services. There are no ads, no advertising ids and no tracking across other apps or websites.

Children

The game is made to be safe for children. The first time the game starts it asks once, in a neutral way, how old the player is. It keeps only whether the player is under 13, never the exact age, and only on the device. For a player under 13 the game turns on a set called Child safety: Friends only (no town square, players who are not friends are not shown or heard), hidden from the Wi-Fi's Nearby list, no usage counts, no pushes while the game is closed, a grown-up's check before becoming friends, crash reports with no friend code or device detail, and a short card about keeping names and places secret. A grown-up can switch the whole set, or any single item, on or off under Settings > Grown-ups > Child safety (behind the grown-up check), and can correct the answer there. It collects no contact details and has no chat, and the only things another player can see are a pet, a number and, for friends and nearby players, a nickname. Kid mode keeps young children in their own simple room: no town square, and becoming friends with someone needs a grown-up's yes. A grown-up can set a daily play limit, quiet hours and school hours, turn on Friends only (no town square, and players who are not friends are not shown or heard), and switch the usage counts off, under Settings > Grown-ups (behind a small grown-up check). Purchases, when the game offers them, sit behind the same check.

For parents. The first thing to check is Settings > Grown-ups > Child safety. We follow the rules for children's privacy where you live, including the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children beyond what this page describes, and we never use it for ads or sell it. As a parent you can ask us at any time to show you what our server holds for your child's game, to delete it, or to stop it being collected: write to support@gotchigalaxy.app with the friend code (Pet Plaza shows it). You can also switch off the usage counts and Friends online yourself, or remove the game, which ends everything it sends.

Keeping and deleting

  • Usage counts are kept for up to 90 days, crash reports for up to 90 days.
  • Leaderboard scores and community goal shares are kept for the season they belong to, then removed.
  • Reports of other players are kept for 1 year.
  • To delete everything on your device, use Settings > Start over, or remove the game.
  • To delete what our server holds for your device, write to support@gotchigalaxy.app with your friend code (Pet Plaza shows it). We delete it within 30 days.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version goes on this page with a new date, and the game's What's new card mentions it.
